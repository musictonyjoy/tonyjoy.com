Work

I sat in the engineer's chair before I sat at the product table.

As an engineer, my job was to make a room full of complex machines run so smoothly that artists forgot I was in it. Fires were out before anyone knew they had started.

Now I bring that experience to the product table, shaping products for post-production and music. I use what I help make, push it until it breaks, then help the teams fix it.

I'm as comfortable with product definition, prompt engineering and generative AI workflows as I am recording a Bösendorfer for a Hollywood film.

I've shaped the tools, and the films and music made with them.

2014

A voice at the fingertips

After trying comparable tools like Vocaloid, I built a patch in Symbolic Sound's Kyma so A.R. Rahman could play a recorded singer's voice on a Haken Continuum, while picking the words of the lyrics from the iPad counterpart of the patch. I designed it with Kyma's designer, Carla Scaletti, and the Continuum's inventor, Lippold Haken.

The Continuum allowed artistic expression in all three dimensions. Left to right was the pitch, and we could set the Y and Z axes to vibrato, filter or portamento. The formants held as the pitch moved, so the voice still sounded human.

2014

Gestures in Pro Tools

Frankensteined Leap Motion into Pro Tools so each finger could control its own track, in banks of ten.

2010

Composing in surround

Engineered A.R. Rahman's room so he could compose in 5.1, when 5.1 was mostly used for mixing.

Alongside his regular stereo instruments, he could play sampled instruments in 5.1 from an iPad, through a Kyma patch I built. Where his fingertip touched the iPad decided where the sound sat in the room, left to right and front to back.

2004

Ringtones with tactile feedback

My prehistoric take on haptics. I composed vibration patterns that followed the bass line, on phones from Sony Ericsson, Motorola, Kyocera and Nokia. Some shipped with DRM I implemented: thirty plays, then the ringtone you'd grown to love locked until you bought it.

The invisible engineer

I sat in the engineer's chair before I sat at the product table.

As a music engineer, my job was to make a room full of complex machines run so smoothly that artists forgot I was in it. Fires were out before anyone knew they had started.

Now I sit at the product table too, shaping products for music and post-production. I use what I help make, push it until it breaks, then help the teams fix it. I'm as comfortable with product definition, prompt engineering and generative AI workflows as I am recording a Bösendorfer for a Hollywood film.

2026

Hands on with generative AI

I work with generative audio tools as they emerge: ElevenLabs, Suno, Moises, AceForge, Google Magenta. During my 2026 residency with Google's Flow Music Sessions, I published an audio plugin, built with AI coding agents and inspired by an Icelandic sea cave and its puffins.

2019

Avid's first Immersive Audio Lab

A full Dolby Atmos room inside a corporate office was a hard sell. I designed and built it from an empty room. It went on to host AES and CAS community events, and about two years later headquarters built a second room modeled on it.

2015

A spatial record, early FAILED

Co-produced at United Recording with Sankar Thiagasamudram, CEO of Audeze. An early attempt at immersive, reactive music production. It failed. I list it anyway.

2014

A voice you can play

Wired Symbolic Sound's Kyma to a Haken Continuum so A.R. Rahman could play a recorded singer's voice as an instrument, bending pitch while the formants held, so it stayed human.

2014

Gestures into Pro Tools

Connected Leap Motion to Pro Tools so natural hand gestures could drive audio production.

2010

Composing in surround

Engineered A.R. Rahman's room so he could compose in 5.1, when 5.1 was mostly reserved for mixing. He triggered sampled instruments from an iPad, each touch placing the sound in the room.

2004

Ringtones you could feel

Vibration patterns composed to follow the bass line, on phones from Sony Ericsson, Motorola, Kyocera and Nokia. Some shipped with DRM I implemented: thirty plays, then the ringtone locked until you bought it.

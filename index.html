The invisible engineer

I sat in the engineer's chair before I sat at the product table.

As a music engineer, my job was to make a room full of complex machines run so smoothly that artists forgot I was in it. Fires were out before anyone knew they had started.

Now I sit at the product table too, shaping products for music and post-production. I use what I help make, push it until it breaks, then help the teams fix it.

I'm as comfortable with product definition, prompt engineering and generative AI workflows as I am recording a Bösendorfer for a Hollywood film.

2026

Generative AI for real‑world productions

I test the latest and greatest generative audio tools like ElevenLabs, Suno, Moises, AceForge and Google Magenta on real productions, and try to break them. Often successfully.

During my 2026 residency with Google's Flow Music Sessions, I published an audio plugin, built with AI coding agents and inspired by an Icelandic sea cave and its puffins.

2019

Avid's first Immersive Audio Lab

A full Dolby Atmos room inside a corporate office was a hard sell. I designed and built the lab from an empty room. It went on to host AES and CAS community events, and about two years later Avid headquarters built a second room modeled on it.

2016

Live concerts

I designed and deployed the rig for A.R. Rahman's performance at TED in 2016. From 2017 to 2019 I ran front of house and every technical side of Vidya Vox's live shows, from the US and India to South Africa and Hong Kong.

2015

A spatial record, early. Status: FAILED

Immersive choral recording. Co-produced at United Recording with Sankar Thiagasamudram, CEO of Audeze, when spatial audio was almost exclusively reserved for sound design. An early attempt at reactive music production. It failed. I list it anyway.

2014

A singer at your fingertips

I built a patch in Symbolic Sound's Kyma so A.R. Rahman could play a recorded singer's voice as an instrument on a Haken Continuum, while picking the phrases from an iPad.

The formants held as the pitch moved, so the voice still sounded human. I worked closely with Kyma's designer, Carla Scaletti, and the Continuum's inventor, Lippold Haken, to design the patch.

2014

Gestures in Pro Tools

I Frankensteined Leap Motion into Pro Tools so each finger could control its own track, in banks of ten.

2010

Composing in surround

I engineered A.R. Rahman's room so he could compose in 5.1, when 5.1 was mostly used for mixing. Alongside his regular stereo instruments, he could play sampled instruments in 5.1 from an iPad, through a Kyma patch I built. Where his fingertip touched the iPad decided where the sound sat in the room, left to right and front to back.

2004

Ringtones with tactile feedback

My prehistoric take on haptics. I composed vibration patterns that followed the bass line, on phones from Sony Ericsson, Motorola, Kyocera and Nokia. Some shipped with DRM I implemented: thirty plays, then the ringtone you'd grown to love locked until you bought it.
